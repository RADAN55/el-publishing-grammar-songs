Name That Noun

Interactive Student Song Sheet
Grades 9-12 | Richard A. Daniel, EL Teacher

1Learn the noun test

A noun names a person, place, thing, or idea. Ask: Who or what is being named?

Personstudent, teacherWho?
Placeschool, LaurelWhere?
Thinglaptop, backpackWhat object?
Ideafreedom, courageWhat thought or feeling?
Perfect-response strategy: Read the whole sentence. Find the word that names something. Classify it. Then explain your answer with evidence from the sentence.

2Listen and follow the lyrics

First listen: enjoy the track. Second listen: notice the noun examples. Open each section as the song plays.

Intro and Verse 1
Dale, Laurel, are you ready? Every word has a job. If it names something, we call it a noun. Uno, dos, tres - let's go! A person like a student, a teacher or a friend, A place like school or Laurel where the learning never ends. A thing can be a laptop, a backpack or a chair, An idea can be freedom, responsibility or care. Read the whole sentence, let the meaning come around, If the word is naming something, then you found yourself a noun.
Pre-Chorus and Chorus
Who? What? Where? Say it right now. Find the name, find the job, name that noun.
Name that noun! What do you see? A person, place, thing or idea it can be. Nombra el sustantivo, say it out loud, Find it in the sentence and identify it proud. Persona! Lugar! Cosa! Idea! If the word names something, it's a noun - ahora!
Verse 2 - Common and Proper
A common noun is general: a city, school or team. A proper noun is special, a particular name we mean. Mississippi, Laurel High - capitalize the name. Start it with a capital; that's the proper-noun game. Common or proper, check the word and look around. Use the clue inside the sentence when you name that noun.
Breakdown and Verse 3
Does student name a person? Yes, student is a noun! Does library name a place? Yes, library is a noun! Does computer name a thing? Yes, computer is a noun! Does freedom name an idea? Yes, freedom is a noun! Concrete is a noun that you can see or hear, A photograph, a ringing bell, a flower growing near. Abstract names an idea you cannot touch or see: Courage, hope and knowledge, respect and honesty. A collective noun names a group: a class, a team, a crowd. Many acting as one - now say the lesson loud!
Bridge and Final Chorus
We plan the celebration - plan is action in that line. The plan is on the table - now a noun is what you find. The same word changes jobs, so check the sentence every time. Name that noun! What do you see? A person, place, thing or idea it can be. Common or proper, concrete or abstract, Use the sentence around it to prove the fact. Persona! Lugar! Cosa! Idea! If the word names something, you have found a noun! Dale, Laurel - name that noun!

6Write a strong response

Prompt: Write one complete sentence that includes a proper noun and an abstract noun. Then explain how you know each word is a noun.

Sentence frame: At __________, our __________ helps students succeed. The first noun is proper because __________. The second noun is abstract because __________.

7Exit ticket

In “The plan is on the table,” what job does plan do?

Which word is an abstract noun?
